# El Paso Land Entitlements Guide

## Key Contacts & Resources

### City of El Paso - Planning & Inspections
- **One Stop Shop**: Primary intake for land development
- **Address**: City Hall, 300 N. Campbell, El Paso TX 79901
- **Phone**: (915) 212-0000 or 3-1-1
- **Hours**: Mon-Fri 8am-5pm
- **Website**: https://www.elpasotexas.gov/planning-and-inspections/

### Recent Texas Law Changes (IMPORTANT - Sept 2025)
1. **SB 840 (Multifamily)**: Allows multifamily/mixed-use in commercial zones with relaxed density, parking, height, lot coverage rules
2. **SB 15 (Small Lot Single-Family)**: Allows small lot SFH on 5+ acre unplatted parcels

**This creates OPPORTUNITIES** - land that wasn't developable before may now be.

## The Entitlement Process (General Steps)

### 1. Due Diligence (Before You Buy)
- [ ] Check current zoning (City GIS maps)
- [ ] Review comprehensive plan (is area planned for growth?)
- [ ] Identify utilities availability (water, sewer, electric)
- [ ] Check flood zone status (FEMA maps)
- [ ] Review any deed restrictions
- [ ] Title search for easements

### 2. Zoning
- **By-Right**: Land already zoned for intended use - just need site plan
- **Rezone Required**: Must apply for zoning change
  - Pre-application meeting (recommended)
  - Submit application + fee
  - Staff review
  - Planning Commission hearing
  - City Council vote (if required)
  - Timeline: 2-6 months

### 3. Platting (Subdividing Land)
- Required when dividing land into lots
- Types:
  - **Minor Plat**: Small subdivisions (typically <4 lots)
  - **Major Plat**: Larger subdivisions
- Process:
  - Submit plat application
  - Technical review (engineering, utilities, drainage)
  - Planning Commission approval
  - Record with County Clerk
- Timeline: 1-3 months

### 4. Site Plan Approval
- Required for commercial, multifamily, or non-residential
- Shows: buildings, parking, landscaping, drainage, utilities
- Reviewed by multiple departments
- Timeline: 4-8 weeks

### 5. Infrastructure
- May need to extend utilities
- Drainage/detention requirements
- Road improvements (possibly)
- Impact fees

### 6. Building Permits
- After entitlements secured
- Submit construction documents
- Pay permit fees
- Inspections during construction

## Finding Deals

### Data Sources
1. **El Paso CAD (Central Appraisal District)**
   - https://www.epcad.org/
   - Property records, ownership, values
   
2. **County Clerk Records**
   - Recent sales, plats, liens
   
3. **Planning Commission Agendas**
   - See what's being approved
   - Track where development is happening

### What to Look For
- Raw land near recent development
- Land with existing utilities nearby (cheaper to connect)
- Properties affected by SB 840/SB 15 (newly developable)
- Distressed owners (estate sales, tax delinquent)
- Land under contract that fell through

### Red Flags
- Flood zones (expensive mitigation)
- No utility access
- Contamination (environmental issues)
- Access problems (landlocked)
- Deed restrictions limiting use

## Deal Structure Ideas

### Option Agreement
- Pay small fee for right to buy at set price
- Use option period to entitle the land
- Either close or walk away
- Risk: option fee only

### Assignment
- Get land under contract
- Assign contract to developer
- Collect assignment fee
- Never actually buy

### Joint Venture
- Partner with developer/capital partner
- You do the entitlement work
- They bring money
- Split profits

### Buy and Entitle
- Buy land outright
- Get entitlements
- Sell to builder at premium
- Highest risk, highest reward
